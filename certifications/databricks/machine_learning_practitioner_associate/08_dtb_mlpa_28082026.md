## Machine Learning Model Deployment

### Model Deployment Strategies

- the process of integrating a machine learning model into a produciton environment
  - make it accessible to end users or other systems to generate predictions or insights
    - generate value
- 4 ways: batch, streaming, real-time, edge/embedded
- deployment and production must handle continously changing new data
  - ongoing monitoring ensures models stay accurate and relevant, feeding back into data collection and retraining as needed
  - feedback loop keeps models effective on production

**Batch** | **Streaming** | **Real-Time** | **Edge/Embedded**
--- | --- | --- | --- 
high throughput | moderate throughput | low latency | on device, ultra low latency, close to the source
results in hours to days | results in seconds to minutes | results in milisseconds | score directly on the device, often offline
scores large volumes of data on a schedule | score records as they arrive continuously | score one request request at a time, on demand | generate predictions locally, close to the data source
compute predictions ahead of time for later use | keep pace with incoming data streams | return predictions within milisseconds | operate with little or no network connection
write results to storage for downstream consumption | write predictions to a sink for immediate use | expose the model behind a live REST endpoint | |

#### Choosing a Strategy

##### The Throughput-Latency Trade-Off

- **throughput:** how many predictions you can produce per unit of time
- **latency:** how quickly a single prediction is available after it is requested
- conflicting goals $\to$ batch maximizes throughput but accepts high latency
  - real-time minimizes latency but scores far less data per call

**Deployment Mode** | **Throughput** | **Latency** | **Use when...**
--- | --- | --- | ---
Batch | high | high (slow response - hours-days) | predictions can be computed ahead of time and consumed later, you need to store large volumes, efficiently and freshness is not required quickly
Streaming | Moderate | Moderate (seconds to minutes) | Data arrives continuously and predictions should keep up within seconds to minutes, but sub-second responses are required
Real-time | Low | Low (miliseconds) | an application needs an answer on demand per request, within miliseconds - a user is waiting on the other end
edge / embedded | Low | Low (depends on device processing power) | predictions must run on the device itself - limited connectivity, strict privacy, or ultra-low latency close to the data source

- the same trained model can deployed in multiple nodes. Deployment strategy is a choice about how predictions are served, based on the use use.
